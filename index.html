Email Inactivity
Survival Guide

A clean, ordered reference for 11 email providers. The central question is simple: before the final inactivity point, can I save the account just by logging in? This guide preserves the providers' actual wording, including where a deadline is discretionary or where no fixed deadline is published.

Official pages checked · 27 August 2026

Executive answer

Best universal maintenance rule
Log into every account at least once every 5 months.

This is a conservative operating interval, not a claim that every provider deletes at five months. It gives you a buffer ahead of Tuta's six-month no-login deadline, the strictest fixed deadline in this group.

What “inactivity” means

There is no universal industry definition. The provider decides which event resets its timer. The safest common practice is an actual sign-in and/or mailbox use, not merely assuming that receiving an email is enough.

Clearly defined activity
Google

Google counts account activity broadly: Gmail, Drive, YouTube, Search, photo sharing, app downloads, and Sign in with Google can all count. Activity is tied to the account, not a particular device.

Proton

Any Proton service counts; Proton gives examples such as opening an email, viewing a file, or connecting to a VPN server.

Aster

Web, desktop, mobile, Bridge, or API activity resets the clock.

Mailbox access is the signal
Tuta

Tuta explicitly says a regular login is necessary to prevent automatic deletion of free accounts.

Zoho

Zoho counts mailbox access through webmail, IMAP, POP, desktop client, or mobile app. Activity elsewhere in Zoho does not substitute for Mail activity.

Not fully enumerated
Mailfence / Autistici / Secria

The cited official material gives an inactivity threshold but does not fully enumerate every event that resets it. Use an explicit account or mailbox login as the least ambiguous maintenance action.

No fixed inactivity deadline located
Disroot / Atomic Mail / Murena

Disroot explicitly says unused accounts do not expire. Atomic says it has no strict inactivity deletion policy. Murena's published 30-day rule concerns setting and verifying a recovery email, not ordinary inactivity.

The rescue strategy

1

Do the check early

Use the five-month cycle. Do not deliberately operate on the provider's last day.

2

Actually sign in

Open the mailbox or use the access method the provider explicitly recognizes. This is more reliable than assuming passive incoming mail counts.

3

If suspended, act immediately

Mailfence and Zoho give meaningful post-threshold windows. Use them rather than waiting.

4

Do not count on recovery after permanent deletion

Once data are purged, treat the mailbox as gone. Provider-specific address recovery is not the same thing as restoring the original mailbox contents.

5

Protect the recovery material

Store passwords, recovery codes and—where applicable—seed phrases or recovery mechanisms in a reputable password manager. A forgotten credential can become a separate failure mode.

6

Back up important correspondence

Inactivity protection is not a backup. Autistici explicitly recommends downloading important mail because it does not keep backups of users' emails.

Simple maintenance calendar

One policy for all 11 accounts
January → June → November → repeat

At each checkpoint, sign into all accounts and mark the check complete. For a newly started cycle, any fixed three dates roughly five months apart work just as well.
